The SF330, explained

Standard Form 330 — "Architect-Engineer Qualifications" — is how architecture, engineering, and related firms present their qualifications to the U.S. federal government. If your firm pursues federal A/E work, the SF330 is your résumé, your portfolio, and your first impression, all in one rigid package.

What the SF330 is

Under the Brooks Act, federal agencies select architect-engineer firms on qualifications rather than price. The SF330 is the standardized instrument for those qualifications. It replaced the old SF254 and SF255 forms in 2004, and it comes in two parts:

  • Part I — Contract-Specific Qualifications. Prepared fresh for each solicitation. It describes the team you are proposing for this contract: the key personnel, their resumes, example projects, and how the team members worked together before.
  • Part II — General Qualifications. A standing profile of a firm (or a branch office): size, disciplines, experience categories, and revenue ranges. Agencies may keep Part II on file, and it is typically updated annually.

Working with the SF330 in Microsoft Word

The government publishes the SF330 as a fillable PDF, but most marketing teams assemble it in Word because resumes and project sheets are living documents that get reused across pursuits. A few hard-won practices:

  1. Build each section as its own template. One Word template for the Section E resume block, one for the Section F project sheet. Assemble per-pursuit copies from those masters rather than editing last year's submission in place. (Our templates guide covers the style-based approach.)
  2. Use tables with fixed row heights to mirror the form's blocks, and keep the block numbering (e.g., "12. Name," "13. Role in this contract") exactly as the form prints it. Evaluators navigate by those numbers.
  3. Keep a master library. Maintain every employee resume and every project description in SF330 format year-round, in one folder, each in the several lengths you actually use (full block, one paragraph, one line). Assembling a pursuit then becomes selection, not writing.
  4. Check the current form edition before every submission. The form is revised occasionally; agencies note the required edition in the solicitation. The authoritative source is GSA's forms library (search "SF330" at gsa.gov/forms), which always hosts the current PDF.
  5. PDF the final package from Word and verify fonts, page count, and file size against the solicitation's instructions. Submissions get rejected on mechanics more often than anyone admits.

Looking for a ready-made SF330 in Word? This site is a reference resource and doesn't sell templates. The current official fillable PDF is free from GSA's forms library, and several commercial vendors sell Word conversions — search "SF330 template Word." The section notes above apply to any of them.

Part II in brief

Part II is a one-page firm profile: employee counts by discipline, experience profile codes with revenue index numbers, and the branch offices it covers. Three things matter in practice: keep it current (agencies may treat a Part II on file as valid for a year), file one per branch office when offices pursue work separately, and make sure the discipline and experience codes you claim are the ones your Part I packages actually demonstrate.
