Why these topics?

For more than twenty years, this domain was home to an Alaska-based technical writing and editing practice whose most-visited pages were about exactly these subjects — the SF330 form in Word, templates for A/E/C firms, and technical editing. That business has moved on, but people still arrive here from old links, bookmarks, and citations looking for the same answers. This site keeps those answers available: the guides are newly written, plain-English treatments of the topics the old site was known for.

Start with the fundamentals

Most business and technical documents fail in the same few ways: they bury the point, they mix formats until the reader loses trust, and nobody edits them before they go out the door. The fixes are boring and reliable:

  • Lead with the conclusion. Reports and proposals are not mystery novels. Put the recommendation, the price, or the finding first, then support it.
  • Use styles, not formatting. In Word, every heading, caption, and body paragraph should carry a named style. Direct formatting is how documents rot. Our templates guide shows the pattern.
  • Separate editing passes. Structure first, sentences second, typos last. A single "read it over" pass catches almost nothing. See the levels of edit.
  • Respect the form. Government solicitations mean what they say. If the RFP says 10 pages, page 11 does not exist. The SF330 guide covers the strictest example.
